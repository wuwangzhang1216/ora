# Ora icon: reference study and revised direction

Status: the first speech-bubble/O concept was rejected by the owner. The light
and dark PNGs in this directory are rejected studies, not a selected design.

## Sources inspected

Read Apple's current icon guidance and visually inspected the actual official
Linear, Raycast, and Craft brand pages. These references inform design judgment;
their protected artwork must not become part of Ora's assets.

| Reference | Observation / lesson for Ora |
| --- | --- |
| [Apple — App icons](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/app-icons) | One clear concept, few shapes, legible small-size edges; consider solid overlapping foreground shapes. Current layered icons separate artwork from system lighting and masking. A gradient is a supporting treatment, not the identity. |
| [Linear — Brand guidelines](https://linear.app/brand) | The diagonal cuts make a simple circle identifiable in monochrome. Its official guidance explicitly asks for generous surrounding space. Ora needs an equally deliberate silhouette before any color or material treatment. |
| [Craft — Official brand assets](https://www.craft.do/brand) | Four asymmetric curved pieces preserve the same recognizable structure in monochrome and dimensional app-icon treatments. Edge light, separation, and coherent surfaces give form; a generic shadow under a flat glyph does not achieve the same result. |
| [Raycast — Press & Media Kit](https://www.raycast.com/press) | The logomark, wordmark, and platform app icon are separately designed assets. A web logo pasted on a square is an incomplete app-icon process. The brand's red accent has a consistent identity across those treatments. |

The visual lessons above are design interpretations, not claims of measurable
superiority or instructions to imitate any reference's protected shape.

## Diagnosis of the rejected Ora studies

- Original: repeated concentric outlines, a center dot, and strong cyan-blue
  compete for attention. It reads as a target/sonar utility.
- Revision: the round bubble and tail read as a Q. The caption strokes add an
  obvious feature illustration without creating a distinctive brand silhouette.
- The proposed light/dark options change color but do not explore different
  identities. Large presentation cards did not improve the actual mark.
- Earlier code-generated material treatment was shallow: a tile gradient plus
  generic drop shadow, without a considered relationship between surfaces.

## New design brief

Personality: a calm, immediate, precise Mac companion for following speech.

Explore a single original shape about two languages passing into one another:
two offset solid curved surfaces with a deliberate negative space. Establish
the silhouette in monochrome first. Do not require the letter O or add a speech
bubble, microphone, waveform bars, or sparkles merely to explain the features.
This is an exploration direction, not an approved final mark.

Then study one restrained material/color treatment with clear lighting and
separation of foreground/background. Use official icon production templates
and inspect compatibility with the app's supported macOS versions.

## Acceptance checks for the next study

1. Recognizable in black-and-white without labels or a gradient.
2. Distinct from common chat/search/assistant icons and the four reference marks.
3. Balanced at 16, 32, 64, 128, and 512 px, with intentional optical adjustments
   where needed; small marks must preserve the same identity.
4. Review in an actual-size Dock and app-list context on both light and dark
   surfaces. A 1024 px poster alone is insufficient.
5. Deliver genuinely different silhouettes for comparison before polishing a
   chosen direction; do not present two palette swaps as two design concepts.

This research does not modify the submitted Ora 0.10.1 (15) build.
